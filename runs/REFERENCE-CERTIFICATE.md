# Reference certificate

**What this is.** For each source cited in *The coordinate ring of the k-fold iterated commutator
locus for 2×2 matrices*, this file records the exact statement the article relies on, quoted or
closely paraphrased from the source itself, together with where it was found and how its access
status was established.

**Why it exists.** The article was written with substantial machine assistance, and fabricated or
misattributed references are a known failure mode of that kind of workflow. The bibliography
itself is written for a reader, and so carries no access-status or verification notes; this file
carries them instead, so that the claim "these sources exist, say what they are cited for, and
were consulted" can be checked rather than taken on trust.

It ships with the arXiv submission as `anc/runs/REFERENCE-CERTIFICATE.md`.

**Status of each entry below** is one of:

- **verified against the source** — the cited text was read in the source itself;
- **verified against publisher metadata** — bibliographic data confirmed via DOI/arXiv/publisher,
  the full text not re-read for this certificate;
- **not independently verified** — stated plainly where that is the case.

## [1] Bruns and Vetter, *Determinantal Rings*, LNM 1327, Springer, 1988

**Status: verified against the source.** Out of print; made freely available with Springer's
permission on the first author's page at
`https://www.home.uni-osnabrueck.de/wbruns/brunsw/detrings.pdf`. That copy was downloaded and read
directly (it is in this project's `literature/`).

Two results are used.

**Theorem (2.7), printed page 13** — cited for: a determinantal ideal at expected grade is perfect.
The statement is given for an **arbitrary** matrix over a Noetherian ring, not only for a matrix of
independent indeterminates. That generality is exactly what the article needs, since one row of its
2×3 matrix is a fixed polynomial vector. Used in Proposition 29, hence in Theorem 31.

**Theorem (16.36), printed page 217** (Appendix 16.F, "The Theorem of Hilbert-Burch") — cited for:
the structure of codimension-2 perfect ideals. As actually printed it gives `I = a · I_m(f)` for a
nonzerodivisor `a`. **It does not give a generator count**, and the article's Remark 30 says so
explicitly; the generator count is proved independently, from linear independence.

*Correction recorded:* an earlier draft mis-cited the second author as "Vasconcelos". Corrected
2026-08-16.

## [2] Eagon and Northcott, *Ideals defined by matrices and a certain complex associated with them*, Proc. Roy. Soc. London Ser. A 269(1337) (1962), 188–204

**Status: verified against publisher metadata** (Crossref, DOI `10.1098/rspa.1962.0170`). The
volume, issue, year and pagination are confirmed. Closed access; the full text was **not** consulted
for this article, and nothing is cited from it beyond the attribution of the original construction.
The result actually used is taken from Bruns–Vetter [1], which was read.

## [3] Motzkin and Taussky, *Pairs of matrices with property L. II*, Trans. Amer. Math. Soc. 80 (1955), 387–401

**Status: not independently verified.** JSTOR-gated and not obtained. Cited only for the classical
irreducibility of the 2×2 commuting variety, which is standard and attributed to this paper
throughout the literature.

*Correction recorded:* an earlier draft additionally cited this paper for the **dimension** of the
generic determinantal variety. That is not plausibly a result of a paper on pairs of matrices with
property L; the citation was moved to the classical codimension formula (Bruns–Vetter) with the
Segre-cone computation given alongside. Corrected 2026-09-04.

## [4] The Stacks Project, tag 00OS

**Status: verified against the source.** Fetched live from
`https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00OS`. Open access. Cited for equicodimensionality of affine
domains over an arbitrary field — a dimension formula for finitely generated field extensions, not
requiring a perfect-field hypothesis. The equivalent statement in Eisenbud is cited alongside it,
as its own reference [14]; until 2026-10-02 the two shared this entry.

## [5] Gerstenhaber, *On dominance and varieties of commuting matrices*, Ann. of Math. 73 (1961), 324–348

**Status: verified against publisher metadata.** Cited for the bound `dim_F F[A,B] ≤ n` for the
subalgebra generated by two commuting matrices, and for irreducibility of the commuting variety —
both standard attributions.

## [11] Youcis, answer to "What can we say about the image of a regular map?", Mathematics Stack Exchange, question 731178, answer 732415, 2014

**Status: verified against the source.** Fetched live; the answer belongs to the stated question and

## [12] The Stacks Project, tag 031R = Lemma 10.157.3

**Status: verified against the source.** Fetched live. Quoted text: *"Let R be a Noetherian ring.
The following are equivalent: (1) R is reduced, and (2) R has properties (R₀) and (S₁)."*

**A trap worth recording**, and the reason this entry is spelled out: the enclosing *section* 10.157
is tag 031O and is titled "Serre's criterion for **normality**", and its own Lemma 10.157.4 (tag
031S) is the normality criterion (R₁) + (S₂) — a different statement. Fetching 031O displays the
whole section including this lemma, so it is easy to confirm the quoted text on a page whose tag is
nevertheless the wrong one.

*Correction recorded:* this entry carried exactly that wrong tag until 2026-08-31.

## [13] Tambour, *The Number of Solutions of Some Equations in Finite Groups and a New Proof of Itô's Theorem*, Comm. Algebra 28 (2000), no. 11, 5353–5361

**Status: verified against the source, via the earlier open-access version.**

The published Taylor & Francis version is **closed access**. Checked 2026-09-04 against Unpaywall
(`api.unpaywall.org/v2/10.1080/00927870008827160`), which reports `is_oa: false`,
`oa_status: "closed"`, and zero OA locations. A T&F link that appears to work may be misleading:
one carrying a `casa_token` parameter is Campus Activated Subscriber Access, i.e. access granted
by a prior institutional session, not open access. (A bare `curl` of the DOI returns 403, but that
proves nothing on its own — publishers serve bot-challenge pages before evaluating entitlement.)

The earlier version **is** freely available, was downloaded and read, and is in this project's
`literature/`:

> Torbjörn Tambour, *On the number of solutions of some equations in finite groups*, Stockholm
> University Research Reports in Mathematics No. 15, 1998, ISSN 1401-5617, 12 pp.
> <https://www2.math.su.se/reports/1998/15/>  (PDF: `1998-15.pdf`)

Note the earlier title is genuinely different — it lacks the "and a new proof of Itô's theorem"
clause — so both are given in the bibliography rather than one being treated as a reprint of the
other. The bibliography cites the published version and points at the report as the retrievable
one.

## [14] Eisenbud, *Commutative Algebra with a View Toward Algebraic Geometry*, Springer GTM 150, 1995

**Status: verified against publisher metadata.** Cited only for Cor. 13.4, that affine domains
over a field are catenary and equicodimensional, alongside the equivalent Stacks Project tag 00OS
[4]. A commercially published textbook, so no copy is held in this project's `literature/` (this
workspace downloads open-access sources only); the statement relied on is standard, and the Stacks
tag, which *was* read directly, carries it independently.

**Provenance of this entry.** Added 2026-10-02. Through every earlier draft and the arXiv
submission of 2026-09-04, Eisenbud and the Stacks Project were a single bundled reference [4],
naming two different sources in one numbered entry. Jan caught it reading the built PDF. Split
into two entries, both cited at the one place the bundled entry was cited.

## What this certificate does not claim

It does not claim that every source was read in full — several are cited for standard attributions
and are marked accordingly above. It records what was checked and what was not, which is the point:
a reader who wants to verify the article's use of the literature can see exactly where to start,
and where the article is relying on a standard attribution rather than on a reading.
