* A wall-time gotcha, not a mathematical one

\(I_k\) has two natural generating sets: the chained-=cross5= entries (what the real algorithm
produces) and the three entries of \(w_k\) (the \(\tau\)-recursion). Line =[0]= of every block
above confirms they generate the *same ideal*. Singular's =primary_decomposition= is nevertheless
extremely sensitive to which one it is given: at \(k=6\) the =cross5= generators decompose in
*61 seconds*, while the \(\tau\)-recursion generators had *still not finished after 15 minutes*
and were killed. The script hands Singular the =cross5= set throughout for this reason. Recorded
here because the obvious way to write this script -- build \(w_k\) by the recursion, decompose it
-- is the slow one, and the failure mode is "it just never returns", not an error.

* What to read out of these

1. Line =[0]= in each block: the ideal produced by the real chained =cross5= code and the ideal
   produced by the \(\tau\)/cross recursion are *equal*, so everything below concerns the actual
   commutator ideal.
2. Line =[3]=: every component of \(I_k\)'s primary decomposition is matched to a \(Y_1\) piece or
   to \(Y_2\) by *exact ideal equality* (both containments via Gröbner bases), not by dimension or
   degree coincidence.
3. Line =[4]=: all containment tests between \(Y_2\) and each \(Y_1\) piece return =False=, in both
   directions, computed by ideal membership of generators — independently of the primary
   decomposition, which is the point (reading non-containment off the decomposition would be
   circular).
4. Line =[5]=: an explicit point where \(w_{k-1}\ne0\), constructed by the same recipe as Lemma 4.2
   of the proof-plan note. Worth noting the naive guess \((e_1,e_2,e_3)\) *fails* at \(k-1=3\)
   (\(w_3=0\) there), which is why the recipe, not a guess, is what the script implements.
