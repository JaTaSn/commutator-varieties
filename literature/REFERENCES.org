Full research corpus — everything that comes up while researching, not just what ends up cited.
Ordered by relevance (most to least), per this workspace's project-level convention (org format,

1. *Bari (2021)*, /On the Structure Tensor of $\mathfrak{sl}_n$/, PhD thesis, Texas A&M
   University (advisor: J.M. Landsberg).
   - File: =bari_structure-tensor-sln-thesis.pdf=.
   - Relevance: *highest* — this is, almost verbatim, our project's own question, already studied
     under the name "structure tensor of $\mathfrak{sl}_n$" (the Lie bracket bilinear map on
     traceless $n\times n$ matrices — loses nothing vs. the full commutator, since scalar/trace
     parts never contribute to $[A,B]$ anyway).
   - Included: cited throughout =working-notes/small-commutators-musings.org=.
   - Downloaded: yes — legitimate open academic posting, hosted directly on advisor J.M.
     Landsberg's own Texas A&M page.
   - Summary: proves $\mathbf R(T_{\mathfrak{sl}_2})=5$ (citing Mirwald 1985) and new bounds
     $16\le\mathbf R(T_{\mathfrak{sl}_3})\le20$ (the lower bound via a new "border apolarity"
     technique — first known explicit tensor with border rank $\ge2m$ for $m<13$; the upper bound
     via a numerical search giving an explicit, if algebraically messy, rank-20 decomposition using
     a primitive 6th root of unity) and a border-rank lower bound $\mathbf R(T_{\mathfrak{sl}_4})
     \ge28$. Crucially states (Theorem 4.1, attributed to Lim & Ye below) that the *asymptotic
     exponent* of $\mathfrak{sl}_n$'s structure tensor equals $\omega$, the ordinary
     matrix-multiplication exponent, exactly — so upper bounds on $\mathbf R(T_{\mathfrak{sl}_n})$
     are also upper bounds on $\omega$ itself, and there is no separate asymptotic "commutator
     speedup" to be found (see the working note's dedicated section on this). Techniques used
     (Koszul flattenings, border substitution, border apolarity) are representation-theoretic/
     algebraic-geometric and fairly heavy — noted as a ceiling on how far we can extend these
     specific bounds ourselves without the same machinery.

2. *Mirwald (1985)*, /The algorithmic structure of $\mathfrak{sl}(2,k)$/, Proc. 3rd International
   Conference on Algebraic Algorithms and Error-Correcting Codes (AAECC-3), Springer, 274–287.
   - Relevance: *highest for the $n=2$ case* — the actual original source for
     $\mathbf R(T_{\mathfrak{sl}_2})=5$, cited by Bari above. Predates Gustafson–Aluru (below) by
     11 years and is specifically about $\mathfrak{sl}(2,k)$, not the cross product per se.
   - Included: cited in the working note as the authority for "5 is optimal," via Bari's citation.
   - Downloaded: no — old Springer LNCS conference proceedings, not found freely available;
     not pursued further (low expected yield for an 11-page 1985 conference paper).
   - Summary: not read directly (only via Bari's citation) — flagged if we ever want the original
     algorithm as actually written, rather than our own independently-rediscovered one.

3. *Gustafson & Aluru (1996)*, /Massively Parallel Searching for Better Algorithms or How to Do a
   Cross Product with Five Multiplications/, Scientific Programming 5(3), 203–217.
   - Relevance: *high* — independent confirmation that the 3D cross product (= the $2\times2$
     commutator, traceless part) has bilinear complexity 5, found by a genuinely different method
     (their own massively-parallel brute-force search program, one of the field's early examples of
     automated algorithm discovery — a spiritual ancestor of AlphaTensor/AlphaEvolve, which is what
     produced this project's founding DPS reference).
   - Included: cited in the working note.
   - Downloaded: no — Scientific Programming (IOS Press) journal article; found only via
     ResearchGate abstract page (login-gated) and a probably-stale Ames Laboratory URL
     (=scl.ameslab.gov=, DOE national lab); not pursued further. A DOE/Ames Laboratory technical
     report version may exist via OSTI and be genuinely open access — not checked.
   - Summary: not read directly — we independently rediscovered a (probably different, equally
     valid) 5-multiplication formula ourselves by a bounded randomized local search rather than
     transcribing theirs; see =code/search_5mult_cross_product.py= and
     =code/verify_2x2_commutator_5mult.sage=.

4. *Bruns & Vetter (1988)*, /Determinantal Rings/, Lecture Notes in Mathematics 1327,
   Springer-Verlag.
   - Relevance: *highest, as of 2026-08-16* — supplies the exact classical theorems this project's
     own results (Conjecture 1's Cohen–Macaulayness/generator-count, and the codimension input to
     Conjecture 2's Theorem 4.6) rest on: Theorem (2.7) (p. 13), the general "grade at expected
     value $\Rightarrow$ perfect" result for the ideal of maximal minors of an *arbitrary* matrix
     (not just independent-indeterminate entries — exactly this project's situation, where one row
     of the relevant $2\times3$ matrix is a fixed complicated polynomial vector, not a row of
     indeterminates); and Theorem (16.36) (p. 217, Appendix 16.F, "The Theorem of Hilbert-Burch"),
     the codimension-2 structure theorem itself, with an explicit remark that its hypotheses hold
     whenever the ambient ring is a polynomial ring over a field, $\mathrm{grade}(I)=2$, and $A/I$
     is Cohen–Macaulay — again, precisely this project's situation. Section 2.C is the actual
     construction of the Eagon–Northcott complex (pp. 16–22).
   - Included: cited as =BrunsVetter1988= in =article/fast-commutators.typ='s Proposition 3,
     replacing an earlier, less careful citation to "Bruns–Vasconcelos" (a misremembered co-author
     — Vetter, not Vasconcelos; caught and fixed 2026-08-16 during this citation chase).
   - Downloaded: *yes* — genuinely open access: the book is out of print, and the title page states
     "The authors are grateful to Springer-Verlag for the permission to make this postscript file
     accessible," hosted directly on Bruns' own Universität Osnabrück page. Confirmed by fetching
     and inspecting the PDF directly (title page, table of contents, and both theorem statements
     checked verbatim) rather than trusted from a search snippet.
   - Summary: as above — read directly (the two specific theorems and their surrounding
     subsections), not just cited secondhand.
   - File: =bruns-vetter1988_determinantal-rings.pdf=.

5. *Eagon & Northcott (1962)*, /Ideals defined by matrices and a certain complex associated with
   them/, Proc. Roy. Soc. London Ser. A 269(1337), 188–204. DOI: 10.1098/rspa.1962.0170.
   - Relevance: *high* — the historical origin of the Eagon–Northcott complex, and (per the
     2026-08-16 direct read below) actually sufficient *on its own* for this project's Proposition
     21/Theorem 23, not merely the superseded predecessor of Bruns–Vetter's treatment.
   - Included: cited as =EagonNorthcott1962= in =article/ring-of-k-commuting-2x2-matrices.typ='s
     Proposition 21.
   - Downloaded: *yes*, 2026-08-16 — closed access via Unpaywall (confirmed, no OA location for the
     DOI), but obtained via JSTOR through Jan's LiU institutional access after he dropped a copy in
     =INBOX/=; filed at
     =literature/subscription/eagon-northcott1962_ideals-defined-by-matrices.pdf= (not the main
     =literature/= directory, per the workspace's subscription-provenance convention).
   - Summary: *read in full*, 2026-08-16, prompted by Jan asking specifically what this project
     uses from it. Correction to the earlier "superseded... arbitrary matrix entries, not just the
     generic case" framing above (which implied the 1962 theorem itself needed genericity): it does
     not. Theorem 2 (§5, p. 201) is already fully general — arbitrary Noetherian $R$, arbitrary
     $s\times r$ matrix $A$, no independent-indeterminates hypothesis anywhere — and gives exactly
     $\mathrm{pd}(R/(A))=r-s+1$ (with an explicit resolution) whenever
     $\mathrm{gr}\{(A);R\}=r-s+1$. Applied directly to $I_k$ ($s=2,r=3$, expected grade 2, matching
     this project's own Theorem 15/codim-2 result), this already gives Theorem 23's
     Cohen–Macaulayness via Auslander–Buchsbaum, with no need for Bruns–Vetter's more general
     hypotheses claim (Bruns–Vetter remains fine as a modern-textbook citation, just not necessary
     to cover a gap that turns out not to exist). Full working-through, including an explicit $I_3$
     example unwinding the Eagon–Northcott complex's own graded pieces to recover the
     3-generators/2-syzygies Hilbert–Burch shape directly:
     =working-notes/eagon-northcott-1962-primary-source.org=. Not yet reflected in the article
     itself — flagged there as a citation-accuracy finding for Jan to decide on, not acted on
     unilaterally.

6. *Ja'Já (1978)*, /Optimal Evaluation of Pairs of Bilinear Forms/, STOC 1978, 173–183 (journal
   version: SIAM J. Computing, 1979).
   - Relevance: *medium* — foundational general theory for exactly this class of problem
     (simultaneously evaluating a small fixed set of bilinear forms with minimal nonscalar
     multiplications — matrix, quaternion, and other structured products are all instances), giving
     a complete solution for the two-bilinear-form case via Kronecker pencil theory. Our commutator
     problem is a *three*-bilinear-form instance (not two), so this doesn't directly apply, but is
     the right classical entry point for the general theory.
   - Included: not cited yet.
   - Downloaded: no — ACM STOC 1978 proceedings / SIAM J. Computing, both paywalled; not found
     open access.
   - Summary: not read directly, only via search-result abstracts.

7. *Lim & Ye (2020)*, /Ubiquity of the exponent of matrix multiplication/, ISSAC 2020, 8–11.
   - Relevance: *high* — the actual source (per Bari's Theorem 4.1 citation) of the exponent-
     equivalence result: $\omega$ is the asymptotic exponent not just for ordinary matrix
     multiplication but for the product operation in whole families of Lie, Jordan, and Clifford
     algebras, structure tensor of $\mathfrak{sl}_n$ included. Directly resolves this project's
     "block-recursion obstruction" open question from session 1 (see the working note).
   - Included: cited (via Bari) in the working note.
   - Downloaded: no — ACM Digital Library only, paywalled; no self-archived copy found on either
     author's homepage (Ke Ye, Lek-Heng Lim both checked). /LiU likely has ACM DL access via
     institutional subscription/ — if Jan wants the actual paper rather than Bari's summary of its
     one relevant theorem, this is a candidate for =literature/subscription/= once fetched through
     his own login, not something an agent can obtain autonomously.
   - Summary: not read directly, only via Bari's citation of its Theorem 4.1.

8. *Conner, Gesmundo, Landsberg & Ventura (2020)*, /Kronecker Powers of Tensors and Strassen's
   Laser Method/, ITCS 2020 (LIPIcs vol. 151), arXiv:1909.04785.
   - Relevance: *medium* — general theory of exactly the recursive/self-tensoring mechanics
     (Kronecker powers of a tensor, and what does or doesn't happen to rank/border rank under
     repeated tensor-squaring) that underlies whether a small commutator decomposition can be
     "recursed" the way Strassen's can. Cited in Bari's own references list ([23]); not yet read in
     depth ourselves.
   - Included: not cited yet.
   - Downloaded: yes — =conner-gesmundo-landsberg-ventura2020_kronecker-powers-laser-method.pdf=
     (arXiv preprint, openly distributable).
   - Summary: not read beyond the abstract/search-result level yet — flagged as the natural next
     read if we want to understand the general Kronecker-power machinery ourselves rather than just
     cite Lim–Ye's conclusion secondhand. Headline results (from search, not full reading): shows
     border rank of the Kronecker square of the "little Coppersmith–Winograd" tensor equals the
     square of the tensor's own border rank (for $q>2$), and similar for the cube ($q>4$); also
     floats the $3\times3$ determinant tensor as a candidate for Strassen's laser method.

9. *de Groote & Heintz (1985)*, /A lower bound for the bilinear complexity of some semisimple Lie
   algebras/, in Algebraic algorithms and error-correcting codes (Grenoble, 1985), LNCS 229,

   - Relevance: *medium* — per Bari's Introduction, this was "the only known non-trivial result" on
     $\mathbf R(T_{\mathfrak{sl}_n})$ before her own thesis — i.e. the direct predecessor result to
     what we actually want (rank bounds for $\mathfrak{sl}_n$, $n\ge3$).
   - Included: not cited yet.
   - Downloaded: no — old Springer LNCS conference proceedings, not found freely available.
   - Summary: not read directly, only via Bari's citation.

10. *Dumas, Pernet & Sedoglavic (2025)*, /A non-commutative algorithm for multiplying a 3×4 matrix
   by a 4×7 matrix using 63 non-complex multiplications/, HAL hal-05121550v2.
   - Relevance: *high — the project's founding reference*, but not itself about commutators; its

   - Summary: see =STATE.org= for the full summary (unchanged from session 1).

** Commuting-matrices ring/variety literature (added 2026-08-16, for the $[A,B,C]{=}0$ variety analysis)

Jan asked specifically for Freyja Hreinsdóttir's work on "the ring of commuting matrices" and what
she cites — directly relevant background for
=working-notes/varieties-of-triple-commutators.org='s analysis of $\{(A,B,C):[A,B,C]=0\}$, which
turns out to be closely parallel to (though a different object from) the classical *commuting
variety* $\{(X,Y):XY=YX\}$ her whole body of work studies.

11. *Hreinsdóttir (2005)*, /Conjectures on the ring of commuting matrices/, arXiv:math/0501465.
   - Relevance: *high* — her most detailed single paper on the subject; studies $R=S/I$ where $I$
     is generated by the entries of $Z=XY-YX$ for generic $n\times n$ $X,Y$, with conjectures on
     syzygies, the canonical module, and Betti numbers, building on her own thesis and 1998 paper.
   - Included: cited in =working-notes/varieties-of-triple-commutators.org=.
   - Downloaded: yes — =hreinsdottir_conjectures-ring-commuting-matrices.pdf= (arXiv, open access).
   - Summary: read in full. States the classical irreducibility results (Motzkin–Taussky,
     Gerstenhaber — both below) as the starting point, then develops first-syzygy conjectures for
     $I$ via a trace-pairing trick ($\mathrm{tr}(A(XY-YX))=0$ whenever $A$ commutes with $X$ or
     $Y$), verified computationally (Macaulay/Macaulay2) through $n=4$–$7$. Rich source of the
     "off-diagonal ideal $J$ vs. full ideal $I$" distinction that directly parallels this project's
     own trace-zero-vs-full-commutator structure.
12. *Hreinsdóttir (1997)*, /On the ring of Commuting Matrices/, PhD thesis, Stockholm University.
    - Relevance: *high* — the founding thesis behind the whole line of work above.
    - Included: cited (bibliographically) in the working note.
    - Downloaded: no — not found freely available; Stockholm University's DiVA portal didn't
      surface a direct link in the time spent searching.
    - Summary: not read directly, only via her later papers' self-citations.
13. *Hreinsdóttir (1998)*, /The Koszul Dual of the Ring of Commuting Matrices/, Communications in
    Algebra 26(11), 3807–3819.
    - Relevance: medium — companion structural result (Koszul duality of $R$) to the syzygy/
      Betti-number work above.
    - Downloaded: no — Taylor & Francis, paywalled.
14. *Hreinsdóttir (2000)*, /The Koszul dual of the ring of three commuting matrices/, Mathematica
    Scandinavica 87(2), 161–199.
    - Relevance: *high* — the natural three-matrix generalization (all of $X,Y,Z$ pairwise
      commuting, a genuinely different object from this project's $[[A,B],C]=0$ — see the working
      note for the distinction). Shows the Koszul dual is the enveloping algebra of a nilpotent Lie
      algebra for $n\ge3$.
    - Downloaded: no — journal states articles $>5$ years old are open access, but the specific
      article/DOI (10.7146/math.scand.a-14304) resolved to a broken/mismatched entry after a
      platform migration (mscand.dk → journals.msp.org); not obtained despite several attempts.
15. *Hreinsdóttir (2006)*, /Miscellaneous results and conjectures on the ring of commuting
    matrices/, Analele Științifice ale Universității "Ovidius" Constanța, Ser. Mat. 14(2), 45–60.
    - Relevance: medium. Downloaded: no — not located as a direct PDF.
16. *Hreinsdóttir (2006)*, /An improved term ordering for the ideal of commuting matrices/, J.
    Symbolic Computation 41(9), 999–1003.
    - Relevance: medium (computational technique, not structural). Downloaded: no — Elsevier,

17. *Motzkin & Taussky (1955)*, /Pairs of matrices with property L. II/, Trans. Amer. Math. Soc.
    80, 387–401.
    - Relevance: *high* — one of the two classical sources (with Gerstenhaber below) proving the
      ordinary commuting variety $\{(X,Y)\in M_n\times M_n:XY=YX\}$ is irreducible.
    - Downloaded: no — not found freely available (old AMS Transactions issue).
18. *Gerstenhaber (1961)*, /On dominance and varieties of commuting matrices/, Ann. of Math. 73,
    324–348.
    - Relevance: *high* — the other classical irreducibility source, and origin of the
      $\dim_k k[X,Y]\le n$ bound for the algebra generated by two commuting $n\times n$ matrices
      (the "Gerstenhaber Problem" for triples, below, asks whether this extends to three).
    - Downloaded: no — Annals of Mathematics, JSTOR-gated, not found freely available.
19. *Knutson (2003)*, /Some Schemes Related to the Commuting Variety/, arXiv:math/0306275.
    - Relevance: high — proves the off-diagonal entries of $XY-YX$ form a regular sequence (cited
      by Hreinsdóttir 2005 above); studies minimal primes of the corresponding ideal.
    - Downloaded: yes — =knutson2003_schemes-related-to-commuting-variety.pdf= (arXiv, open
      access). Not read beyond the abstract/Hreinsdóttir's citation of it yet.
20. *Cherny, Satriano & Song (2024)*, /On the algebra generated by three commuting matrices:
    combinatorial cases/, arXiv:2402.16334.
    - Relevance: high — recent, active-research extension of Gerstenhaber's theorem to *triples* of
      pairwise-commuting matrices (the "Gerstenhaber Problem"), a different three-matrix
      generalization from this project's own $[[A,B],C]$ but a useful point of comparison.
    - Downloaded: yes — =cherny-satriano-song2024_algebra-three-commuting-matrices.pdf= (arXiv,
      open access). Not read in depth yet.
21. *Holbrook & O'Meara (2020)*, /A computing strategy and programs to resolve the Gerstenhaber
    Problem for commuting triples of matrices/, arXiv:2006.08588.
    - Relevance: medium — companion computational-search paper to the above, looking for a
      counterexample to the triples generalization of Gerstenhaber's bound.
    - Downloaded: yes — =gerstenhaber-problem-computing-strategy2020.pdf= (arXiv, open access). Not
      read in depth yet.

22. *Artin & Hochster*, unpublished/attributed conjecture: for $I$ the ideal generated by entries
    of $XY-YX$, $\mathrm{Spec}(R/I)$ is reduced and Cohen-Macaulay.
    - Relevance: *high* — this is precisely the open conjecture Hreinsdóttir's papers (entries
      9–14) are chipping away at case-by-case ($n=3,4$); ties the whole cluster together.
    - Found 2026-08-16 after Jan asked whether Hochster (or Artin) first studied this in the
      1940s. *Correction*: no 1940s source exists — this is *Michael* Artin (algebraic geometer,
      b. 1934, son of Emil Artin, not Emil himself) and Melvin Hochster (commutative algebraist,
      b. 1943, PhD 1967), so nothing by either could predate roughly the late 1960s at the very
      earliest; searching turned up no single dateable original publication at all — it appears to
      circulate purely as an attributed conjecture (oral/informal), cited as "Conjecture 1.1
      (M. Artin, M. Hochster)" with no bracket-reference in Majidi-Zolbanin & Snapp below, i.e. not
      traced to a specific paper even by the people citing it.
    - Downloaded: no (nothing to download — not a paper).
23. *Majidi-Zolbanin & Snapp*, /A note on the variety of pairs of matrices whose product is
    symmetric/ (undated preprint).
    - Relevance: high — the source that states the Artin–Hochster conjecture precisely (as
      "Conjecture 1.1"), and situates it among Motzkin–Taussky, Gerstenhaber, Guralnick (1992,
      not obtained), and Hreinsdóttir's own 1994 paper. Studies a related but different variety
      (pairs whose *product* is symmetric, not pairs that commute), proving it irreducible, reduced,
      and a complete intersection — by contrast with the commuting variety's Cohen–Macaulayness
      still being open in general.
    - Included: cited in this entry.
    - Downloaded: yes — =majidi-zolbanin-snapp_variety-matrices-symmetric-product.pdf= (self-hosted
      on the Ohio State University math department's own web space, standard academic
      self-archiving).
    - Summary: read in full. Also references C. Mueller's 2007 Michigan PhD thesis (advisor
      Hochster) on the closely related "product is symmetric" variety, self-archived at
      =math.lsa.umich.edu/~hochster/mueller.thesis.pdf= — not fetched (that specific host didn't
      resolve when tried), a candidate for a future pass if this thread gets picked up again.

24. *Chau (2024)*, /The $F$-regularity of algebraic sets related to commutator matrices/, Journal
    of Algebra 652, 52–66.
    - Relevance: *high* — Jan found this one himself (dropped in =INBOX/=, 2026-08-16) while
      thinking through the Artin–Hochster/Hochster attribution question above; directly continues
      that thread with real, recent (2024) results.
    - Included: cited in this entry.
    - Downloaded: yes, but *not open access* — =literature/subscription/
      chau2024_f-regularity-commutator-matrices.pdf=. Confirmed closed via Unpaywall
      (=is_oa: false=, no OA locations) before filing; obtained through Jan's own LiU institutional
      access, hence =subscription/= not the main =literature/= directory, per the top-level
      =CLAUDE.md= convention.
    - Summary: read in full. Studies the commutator matrix $C=XY-YX$ of two generic $n\times n$
      matrices and three associated ideals: $\mathfrak d$ (diagonal entries), $\mathfrak a$
      (anti-diagonal), $\mathfrak c=\mathfrak d+\mathfrak a$ (cross-diagonal). Proves $k[X,Y]/
      \mathfrak c$ is $F$-regular for every $n$, and $k[X,Y]/\mathfrak a$ is $F$-regular exactly
      when $n\ne2$ — settling conjectures of Kadyrsizova and Yerlanov (2018, 2022, neither
      obtained yet — see below). Gives a **genuinely dateable correction** to the Artin–Hochster
      question above: states in the introduction, again with no bracketed citation to an actual
      paper, "Hochster (1984) conjectured that these algebraic sets are reduced" — a distinct,
      solo Hochster conjecture (about reducedness, not Cohen–Macaulayness), attached to a real year
      (1984) even though still not traced to an actual publication. Much closer to what Jan
      half-remembered than the 1940s — plausibly the actual source of the recollection.
